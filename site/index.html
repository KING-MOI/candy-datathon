Why fruit looks bad raw but positive here

Fruit candies win fewer points than the rest, but most of "the rest" is chocolate. What doesn't matter as much

A higher price doesn't buy votes. Pricier candy wins because it's usually chocolate.

Across all candies, price and win rate rise together (r = ). Among chocolate candies alone the link is weak (r = , p = ). With ingredients held fixed, price has no clear effect, so the Crispy Cup can sit at a middle price. Sugar is the reverse: it helps a little even after ingredients.

    Method and tests

    Data. candy-data.csv from Kaggle (FiveThirtyEight, 2017): 85 rows, 9 yes/no traits, sugar and price percentiles, and win rate from about 269,000 matchups. We dropped the dime and quarter from every model and kept them as reference lines.

    Raw gaps. Mean win rate with and without each trait, with 5,000-draw bootstrap intervals and 20,000-shuffle permutation tests.

    Second model. A random forest (500 trees, at least 3 candies per leaf) reached leave-one-out R² = . A forest only predicts from combinations it has seen, so it can't reward a pairing no candy has tried. That makes it a check on the regression's guesses about untried pairs.
